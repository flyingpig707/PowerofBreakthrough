# Editorial framework

Use this reference when deciding whether and how a contribution fits 《破圈力》.

## The book's central question

In an AI-mediated communication environment, how can a city move beyond one-off visibility and build a repeatable capability to be perceived, understood, trusted, chosen, and continuously improved?

## Core capability system

| Capability | Central question | Editorial test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 感知力 | 城市能否听见并理解圈外信号？ | Does the proposal improve signal capture, interpretation, or anticipation without treating noisy data as truth? |
| 生成力 | 城市能否持续生产有辨识度的内容？ | Does it strengthen human–AI co-creation, local authenticity, or reusable content assets? |
| 连接力 | 内容能否穿透圈层并形成信任？ | Does it improve audience matching, distribution, verifiable sources, or consistency between claims and experience? |
| 进化力 | 城市能否从每次传播中持续学习？ | Does it create a measurable feedback loop, reusable learning, or a responsible way to adjust strategy? |

The wider architecture is:

```text
认知主权
  ↓
破圈力：感知力 · 生成力 · 连接力 · 进化力
  ↓
组织机制 · 评估体系 · 城市生态
  ↓
可持续的城市价值创造与传播
```

## Fit tests

A useful proposal should clarify a core concept, add verifiable evidence or a boundary, connect one of the four capabilities to a real decision, improve an implementation tool, distinguish short-lived exposure from repeatable capability, or show where the framework needs qualification.

A proposal is not ready when it mainly adds publicity language, repeats the manuscript, invents precise metrics, treats model output or platform popularity as evidence, or promotes a city or vendor without a transferable lesson.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer current primary material: laws and standards, official statistics, original research, audited disclosures, first-party technical documentation, and directly observable public records. Secondary analysis may provide context. Social posts, testimonials, search snippets, AI answers, tourism publicity, and vendor marketing are leads rather than sufficient proof for material claims.

For each important source, capture who published it, what it directly supports, when it was published or retrieved, its geographic and temporal scope, reuse constraints, and limitations.

## Editorial status language

- `proposed`: submitted to the public proposal library, not yet substantively reviewed;
- `under-review`: maintainers are evaluating the proposal;
- `selected`: maintainers intend to incorporate all or part of it, subject to editing;
- `incorporated`: a separate editorial Pull Request has merged material into `book/`.

Do not describe a proposal as author-approved, selected, incorporated, or published unless the repository records that state.
